SECTION 4 · STRATEGY

4.Investment objective and strategy

4.1 Investment objective

The investment objective of the Fund is to generate target returns through a portfolio of US private market positions, deployed via a credit facility secured by LBMA-grade physical gold contributed by Members. The Fund seeks to combine:

  1. the floor preservation of physically-allocated gold at LBMA-grade specifications, held in segregated allocated custody at the Singapore Freeport;
  2. the capital efficiency of a secured credit facility provided by a Singapore institutional bank, sized to up to 75% of the prevailing LBMA value of the Fund's gold collateral pool; and
  3. curated US private market exposure sourced through the Manager's relationship network, including pre-IPO secondary equity, late-stage private credit, and other comparable positions.

4.2 Investment strategy components

(a) Gold collateral pool. The Fund holds LBMA-grade physical gold in segregated custody. The gold serves as the primary collateral for the Credit Facility and as the source of the Fund's floor value.

(b) Credit Facility deployment. The Fund draws on the Credit Facility, secured by the gold collateral pool, and deploys the proceeds into US private market positions selected by the Investment Committee. The Credit Facility is sized to up to 75% of the LBMA value of the gold pool, per the parameters set out in the Fund Agreement and sized to Member commitment level.

(c) US private market portfolio. The Fund's investment portfolio is expected to consist primarily of (i) pre-IPO secondary equity in late-stage private companies, (ii) late-stage private credit, and (iii) selectively, other private market exposures consistent with the Fund's investment objective and risk parameters.

SECTION 5 · GOLD COLLATERAL

5.The gold collateral mechanism

5.1 Specification

All gold acquired by the Fund as the collateral pool must be LBMA good delivery 1-kilogram bars of 999.9 fineness, refined by an LBMA-accredited refiner. Each Bar is uniquely serial-numbered.

5.2 Procurement

The Fund procures Bars at the prevailing LBMA spot price at the time of trade execution. Procurement is executed by the Fund through bullion counterparties on terms approved by the Manager. The procurement process, including the Procurement Buffer, is described in §8.

5.3 Custody and segregation

Bars are held in allocated, segregated custody at the bullion custodian's premises at the Singapore Freeport. Each Bar is uniquely identified by serial number and recorded in the Custodian's allocated records as belonging to the Fund and not co-mingled with the assets of any other person.

5.4 Collateral pool

The Bars are pooled at the Fund level and pledged to the Credit Facility provider as collateral for the secured credit facility. A Member's economic interest in the Fund is a pro-rata interest in the Fund as a whole (including the gold collateral pool and the Credit Facility-deployed portfolio), not a direct ownership interest in any specific Bar. The Fund Agreement sets out the rights of Members in respect of the collateral pool, including in the context of insolvency.

5.5 Insurance

The Bars are insured under the Custodian's standard insurance programme. The Custodian's insurance programme is summarised in Schedule B. Insurance coverage is subject to per-event limits, aggregate limits, deductibles, exclusions, and conditions. Certain perils may be excluded. Insurance is not a guarantee against all risks of loss.

5.6 Insolvency segregation

The Fund's gold collateral pool is held in segregated custody at the Singapore Freeport, separate from the assets of the Manager, the Credit Facility provider, and other counterparties. In the event of an insolvency of the Manager, segregation of the Bars at the Custodian level is intended to permit the Bars to be excluded from the insolvency estate of the Manager, subject to the terms of the custody agreement and applicable Singapore insolvency law. Members should not assume that segregation provides absolute protection against all insolvency outcomes; counsel should review the custody agreement and the relevant insolvency analysis.

5.7 Inspection rights

Members are entitled to the inspection rights in respect of the Fund's gold custody set out in the Fund Agreement, including periodic Auditor confirmations of the Fund's bar inventory.

SECTION 7 · CASH RESERVE

7.The Cash Reserve and Capital Call mechanism

7.1 The Cash Reserve

The Fund maintains a Fund-level cash reserve equal to twenty percent (20%) of the Fund's NAV (the "Cash Reserve"), held in cash and short-duration money-market or equivalent instruments. The Cash Reserve is intended as a defensive buffer against market events affecting the gold collateral pool or the Credit Facility deployment.

Sourcing
The Cash Reserve is funded at the Fund level from the Credit Facility draw allocation. When the Fund draws against the gold collateral pool, the Manager allocates approximately eighty percent (80%) of the drawn proceeds to Fund investments (per §4) and retains approximately twenty percent (20%) of the drawn proceeds in the Cash Reserve. The Cash Reserve is therefore funded from the Credit Facility, not from Member subscription wires. SECTION 8 · PROCUREMENT BUFFER

8.4 Procurement Buffer

The Fund retains up to three percent (3%) of the Member's wire as a Procurement Buffer to cover spot movement during the execution window, dealer spread, bar storage setup, insurance attachment, and bullion execution costs.

Operational mechanic

The Procurement Buffer is held in a Fund-level reconciliation account at the Administrator. It is not netted from the Member's wire prior to gold purchase; rather, the full wired amount is applied to gold procurement at the LBMA spot at execution, and the buffer functions as a Fund-level provision against execution-cost overruns. To the extent actual procurement costs exceed nominal spot, the buffer absorbs the difference; to the extent actual procurement costs are less than nominal spot plus dealer spread, the buffer balance is reconciled annually.

Reconciliation

The buffer balance is reconciled annually by the Auditor against actual procurement costs. The reconciliation is reflected in the Member's annual position statement. Any unused portion of the Procurement Buffer is credited to the Member's pro-rata share of the Cash Reserve.
