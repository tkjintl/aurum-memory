## INSTRUCTIONS

This register captures every risk explicit or implied in the AURUM Century Club Package, Structural Memo, and Member FAQ. The intent is to inherit cleanly into the PPM Risk Factors section, where each entry will be expanded to standard PPM language with:

- **Risk identification** (one sentence headline)
- **Description** (factual mechanism)
- **Quantitative impact** (where measurable)
- **Mitigants** (factual structural protections)
- **Disclosure** (what the LP must accept by subscribing)

PPM Risk Factors typically run 30–60 numbered items. This register currently has **38 entries** organized in 7 categories.

**Voice guardrails (post-hardening):** No "operating history" language; no "tier-1" or "London-market" claims; no "burden / eliminate / replace" trigger words; no specific timing commitments where not strictly bound (e.g., "two business days" → "promptly following wire clearance"). Korean tax specifics generalized to "various Korean regulatory and tax reporting obligations" outside the Memo §3 mapping.

## CATEGORY A · STRUCTURAL & GENERAL

### A1. Newly launched fund vehicle
Aurum Funds is a newly launched structured fund vehicle. Targets and mechanics described in the Onboarding Package, Structural Memo, and PPM are derived from the Manager's structuring of the vehicle; prior performance of comparable structures is not indicative of future results.

### A2. Single-manager risk
The Fund is managed by TACC Pte Ltd, a single manager entity. Operational continuity depends on the Manager's continued ability to source, structure, and administer the Fund's positions.

### A3. Key-man dependency
Sourcing, structuring, and management of the Fund depends materially on a small number of principals at the Manager. Loss of any principal may materially affect the Fund's ability to source new positions or manage existing positions. *Key-man provisions specified in LP agreement.*

### A4. Manager track record
Principals' prior deal experience as direct cross-border placers is not a guarantee of fund-vehicle performance. Past placements were single-deal direct transactions, not pooled-vehicle outcomes.

### A5. Conflicts of interest
The Manager and its principals may have other business interests, including direct deal placement to clients outside the Fund. *Conflict policy and allocation rules specified in LP agreement.*

## CATEGORY B · GOLD COLLATERAL & CUSTODY

### B1. Gold price volatility
The Fund's collateral base is physical gold. Gold price declines reduce loan-to-value headroom against the credit facility and may trigger reserve deployment, capital calls, or in extremis margin calls.

### B2. Bank margin call
The Singapore institutional bank's credit facility is monitored at 80% LTV. Sustained gold price declines that breach this threshold without sufficient reserve replenishment could trigger forced sale of collateral.

### B3. LBMA market disruption
Procurement and disposal of LBMA-grade physical gold depends on continued operation of the LBMA market. Market disruption, refiner default, or accreditation withdrawal could affect the Fund's ability to procure or dispose of gold.

### B4. Custody counterparty risk
Allocated bars are held with a Singapore Freeport bullion custodian. Custodian default, fraud, or operational failure could affect access to allocated gold. *Custody specifics — segregation, allocation evidence, default scenarios — disclosed in custody agreement.*

### B5. Insurance coverage limits
Allocated bars are insured under the custodian's standard insurance programme. *Insurance specifics — per-loss cap, perils covered, exclusions, aggregate limits — disclosed in custody agreement.*

### B6. Procurement window slippage
Gold spot price may move between Indication of Interest submission and trade execution. Members bear this slippage; allocated kilogram amount is determined at LBMA spot at execution. *Procurement timing typically follows wire clearance promptly, subject to market conditions and dealer availability.*

### B7. Bar inventory availability
LBMA refiner inventory may be insufficient to meet Fund procurement needs at scale, particularly during periods of high physical demand. Trade execution may be delayed beyond targeted timing.

## CATEGORY C · CREDIT FACILITY

### C1. Single-bank counterparty exposure
The Fund's credit facility is provided by a single Singapore institutional bank. Withdrawal, refusal to renew, or material change in facility terms could materially affect Fund operations. *Replacement counterparty arrangements addressed in PPM and LPA.*

### C2. SORA rate volatility
The credit facility is SORA-linked floating rate. Sustained rate increases compress net spread on credit-sleeve deployments and may reduce target IRR.

### C3. Facility covenant breaches
The credit facility includes operational and financial covenants. Breach of covenants may accelerate the facility, trigger margin calls, or require forced disposal of collateral.

### C4. Facility renewal risk
The credit facility is subject to periodic renewal. Failure to renew on substantially similar terms could materially affect Fund mechanics.

### C5. Cross-default scenarios
Default under the credit facility may interact with default scenarios at the LP level (capital call default by Members) under cross-default provisions to be specified in the LP agreement.

## CATEGORY D · UNDERLYING POSITIONS

### D1. Pre-IPO liquidity delay
Pre-IPO equity positions are illiquid. Liquidity events may be delayed indefinitely; secondary market exits may not be available at acceptable prices. The Fund does not represent any specific liquidity timing for any underlying position.

### D2. Private credit default
Mid-market private credit positions carry default risk. Defaults reduce realized credit-sleeve returns; credit-sleeve gross targets are not guaranteed and depend on deal selection and rate environment.

### D3. Counterparty default on direct-LP investments
The Fund participates in pre-IPO equity through direct-LP and counterparty relationships. Default or material change at the underlying GP, sponsor, or counterparty may affect the Fund's investment.

### D4. Currency exposure on US private investments
US private positions are denominated in USD. KRW/USD exchange rate movements affect Member outcomes when distributions are converted to KRW.

### D5. Position concentration
The Fund's opening book targets two positions at material per-position size relative to opening NAV. Concentration is structured at the Fund level; Member exposure is pro-rata, not direct. *Concentration limits in LP agreement / fund operating policy.*

### D6. Deal sourcing dependency
The Fund's pipeline depends on the Manager's continued access to direct-LP and counterparty relationships in US private markets. Disruption to these relationships could affect deal flow.

### D7. Sector concentration in opening positions
The opening book targets frontier AI infrastructure and defense autonomy sectors. Sector-specific risk applies to early Fund vintages.

### D8. Named opening positions — closing risk
Allocations described in marketing materials for named opening positions are targets subject to deal closing. Allocations may be reduced, restructured, or fail to close depending on counterparty terms, due diligence outcomes, and final allocation availability.

## CATEGORY E · CAPITAL CALL & LIQUIDITY

### E1. Contingent capital commitment
Subscription involves a contingent additional capital commitment beyond the initial subscription, exercisable by the Fund under defined conditions of the LP agreement. *Cap on aggregate Member exposure, minimum notice period, maximum frequency, conditions precedent, and default consequences will be specified in the LP agreement.*

### E2. Capital call frequency / cumulative exposure
Multiple capital calls may be issued over the Fund term. *Cumulative Member exposure cap to be specified in LP agreement.*

### E3. Member capital-call default
A Member who fails to fund a capital call is subject to default consequences. *Default ladder — late-payment interest, dilution mechanics, forced sale of LP interest at NAV less penalty, forfeiture conditions, and cure period — specified in LP agreement.*

### E4. LP interest illiquidity
LP interests are illiquid during the Fund term. Secondary transfers may be considered case by case but are subject to Manager consent and LP agreement transfer restrictions; secondary sale is not guaranteed.

### E5. Term extension requirement
The Fund's base term is 5–7 years; extensions beyond the base term require LP consent. The Manager cannot unilaterally extend. Members should commit only capital they can hold for the full term.

## CATEGORY F · REGULATORY & TAX

### F1. Singapore VCC registration
TACC Pte Ltd intends to register an umbrella VCC prior to first close, with Aurum Funds established as a sub-fund. Failure or delay in registration could affect Fund launch timing or structure.

### F2. Singapore regulatory changes
Singapore fund regulation (SFA, MAS frameworks) may change during the Fund term. Material changes could affect Fund operations or Member outcomes.

### F3. Korean private placement compliance
Distribution of Fund materials to Korean residents is contemplated solely on a reverse-solicitation basis or through a Korean-licensed distributor. Failure to comply with Korean private placement rules could affect Member subscriptions and is subject to Korean securities-regulatory inquiry risk.

### F4. Korean tax law changes
Korean tax law (LCITA, FETA, FSCMA, KoFIU regulations) may change during the Fund term. Changes affecting overseas account reporting, capital gains taxation, or remittance rules may affect Member outcomes.

### F5. Withholding tax & double tax treaty
US private market distributions may be subject to US withholding tax. Singapore-Korea double tax treaty interpretation may affect ultimate Member tax outcomes.

### F6. KoFIU AML / source-of-funds review
Initial Member remittances trigger AML / source-of-funds review at the Member's Korean bank. Adverse findings could delay or block subscription.

### F7. Designated FX bank relationship
Members must designate a Korean foreign exchange bank for Fund-related transactions. Disruption to this relationship affects Member ability to remit, receive distributions, and report.

## CATEGORY G · OPERATIONAL & MISCELLANEOUS

### G1. Fund administrator dependency
NAV calculation and LP reporting depend on an independent Singapore administrator. Administrator failure could affect reporting cadence.

### G2. Auditor dependency
Annual audit depends on an independent Singapore auditor. Audit qualification or delay could affect Member confidence and regulatory standing.

### G3. Cybersecurity & data breach
Fund operations and Member portal access depend on technology systems. Cyber incidents could affect operational continuity, Member data, or transaction execution.

### G4. Force majeure
War, pandemic, sanctions, market closures, and other force majeure events could affect Fund operations, particularly gold procurement and US private market access.

### G5. Litigation risk
The Manager, the Fund, custodians, and counterparties may face litigation that could affect Fund operations, Member outcomes, or Fund standing.

### G6. Reputation risk
Adverse publicity affecting the Manager, the Fund, or any underlying position counterparty could affect Fund operations or Member confidence.

### G7. Information lock-up & MNPI
Members may receive information about underlying positions that constitutes material non-public information under US, Korean, or other applicable securities laws. Members must comply with restrictions on use of such information.

### G8. Side-letter parity
The Manager may enter into side letters with certain Members. *Side-letter policy to be determined and disclosed in the LP agreement.*

### G9. Distribution waterfall mechanics
Distributions are subject to the Fund's distribution waterfall. *Waterfall mechanics — preferred return / hurdle rate, GP catch-up rate, carry split — specified in LP agreement (intended structure: 2/20 with hurdle and catch-up).*
