## CATEGORY A · STRUCTURAL & GENERAL

### A1. New fund vehicle
Aurum Funds is a newly launched structured fund vehicle. The Fund has no operating history. Targets and mechanics described in marketing materials are derived from the Manager's structuring of the vehicle and have not been tested in operating conditions.

### A2. Single-manager risk
The Fund is managed by TACC Pte Ltd, a single manager entity. Operational continuity depends on the Manager's continued ability to source, structure, and administer the Fund's positions.

### A3. Key-man dependency
Sourcing, structuring, and management of the Fund depends materially on a small number of principals at the Manager. Loss of any principal may materially affect the Fund's ability to source new positions or manage existing positions.

### A4. Manager track record
Prior deal experience of the principals is not a guarantee of fund-level performance. Past placements were single-deal direct transactions, not fund-vehicle outcomes.

### A5. Conflicts of interest
The Manager and its principals may have other business interests, including direct deal placement to clients outside the Fund. Conflict policy and allocation rules are set out in the LP agreement.

## CATEGORY B · GOLD COLLATERAL & CUSTODY

### B1. Gold price volatility
The Fund's collateral base is physical gold. Gold price declines reduce loan-to-value headroom against the credit facility and may trigger reserve deployment, capital calls, or in extremis margin calls.

### B2. Bank margin call
The Singapore institutional bank's credit facility is monitored at 80% LTV. Sustained gold price declines that breach this threshold without sufficient reserve replenishment could trigger forced sale of collateral.

### B3. LBMA market disruption
Procurement and disposal of LBMA-grade physical gold depends on continued operation of the LBMA market. Market disruption, refiner default, or accreditation withdrawal could affect the Fund's ability to procure or dispose of gold.

### B4. Custody counterparty risk
Allocated bars are held with a tier-1 Singapore Freeport custodian. Custodian default, fraud, or operational failure could affect access to allocated gold.

### B5. Insurance coverage limits
Allocated bars are insured under London-market specie policies. Insurance limits, exclusions, and per-loss caps are specified in the custody agreement and may not cover all loss scenarios.

### B6. Procurement window slippage
Gold spot price may move between Indication of Interest submission and trade execution. Members bear this slippage; allocated kilogram amount is determined at LBMA spot at execution.

### B7. Bar inventory availability
LBMA refiner inventory may be insufficient to meet Fund procurement needs at scale, particularly during periods of high physical demand. Trade execution may be delayed beyond the targeted two-business-day window.

## CATEGORY C · CREDIT FACILITY

### C1. Single-bank counterparty exposure
The Fund's credit facility is provided by a single Singapore institutional bank. Withdrawal, refusal to renew, or material change in facility terms could materially affect Fund operations. *Replacement counterparty arrangements addressed in the PPM and LPA.*

### C2. SORA rate volatility
The credit facility is SORA-linked floating rate. Sustained rate increases compress net spread on credit-sleeve deployments and may reduce target IRR.

### C3. Facility covenant breaches
The credit facility includes operational and financial covenants. Breach of covenants may accelerate the facility, trigger margin calls, or require forced disposal of collateral.

### C4. Facility renewal risk
The credit facility is subject to periodic renewal. Failure to renew on substantially similar terms could materially affect Fund mechanics.

### C5. Cross-default scenarios
Default under the credit facility may interact with default scenarios at the LP level (capital call default by Members) under cross-default provisions to be specified in the LP agreement.

## CATEGORY D · UNDERLYING POSITIONS

### D1. Pre-IPO liquidity delay
Pre-IPO equity positions have illiquid liquidity profiles, with 18–36 month liquidity horizons. Liquidity events may be delayed indefinitely; secondary market exits may not be available at acceptable prices.

### D2. Private credit default
Mid-market private credit positions carry default risk. Defaults reduce realized credit-sleeve returns; credit-sleeve gross targets are not guaranteed.

### D3. Counterparty default on direct-LP investments
The Fund participates in pre-IPO equity through direct-LP co-investment relationships. Default or material change at the underlying GP or sponsor may affect Fund's investment.

### D4. Currency exposure on US private investments
US private positions are denominated in USD. KRW/USD exchange rate movements affect Member outcomes when distributions are converted to KRW.

### D5. Position concentration
The Fund's opening book targets two positions at material per-position size relative to opening NAV. Concentration is structured at the Fund level; Member exposure is pro-rata, not direct.

### D6. Deal sourcing dependency
The Fund's pipeline depends on the Manager's continued access to direct-LP relationships in US private markets. Disruption to these relationships could affect deal flow.

### D7. Sector concentration in opening positions
The opening book targets frontier AI infrastructure and defense autonomy sectors. Sector-specific risk applies to early Fund vintages.

## CATEGORY E · CAPITAL CALL & LIQUIDITY

### E1. Contingent capital commitment
Subscription involves a contingent additional capital commitment beyond the initial subscription, exercisable by the Fund under defined LP agreement conditions. *Cap, notice, frequency, default consequences specified in LP agreement.*

### E2. Capital call frequency / cumulative exposure
Multiple capital calls may be issued over the Fund term. Cumulative Member exposure is capped per LP agreement.

### E3. Member capital-call default
A Member who fails to fund a capital call is subject to default consequences specified in the LP agreement, which may include late-payment interest, dilution, forced sale of LP interest, and ultimately forfeiture.

### E4. LP interest illiquidity
LP interests are illiquid during the Fund term. Secondary transfers may be considered case by case but are subject to Fund manager consent and are not guaranteed.

### E5. Term extension requirement
The Fund's base term is 5–7 years; extensions beyond the base term require LP consent. Members should commit only capital they can hold for the full term.

## CATEGORY F · REGULATORY & TAX

### F1. Singapore VCC registration
TACC Pte Ltd intends to register an umbrella VCC prior to first close, with Aurum Funds established as a sub-fund. Failure or delay in registration could affect Fund launch timing or structure.

### F2. Singapore regulatory changes
Singapore fund regulation (SFA, MAS frameworks) may change during the Fund term. Material changes could affect Fund operations or Member outcomes.

### F3. Korean private placement compliance
Distribution of Fund materials to Korean residents is contemplated solely on a reverse-solicitation basis or through a Korean-licensed distributor. Failure to comply with Korean private placement rules could affect Member subscriptions.

### F4. Korean tax law changes
Korean tax law (LCITA, FETA, FSCMA, KoFIU regulations) may change during the Fund term. Changes affecting overseas account reporting, capital gains taxation, or remittance rules may affect Member outcomes.

### F5. Withholding tax & double tax treaty
US private market distributions may be subject to US withholding tax. Singapore-Korea double tax treaty interpretation may affect ultimate Member tax outcomes.

### F6. KoFIU AML / source-of-funds review
Initial Member remittances trigger AML / source-of-funds review at the Member's Korean bank. Adverse findings could delay or block subscription.

### F7. Designated FX bank relationship
Members must designate a Korean foreign exchange bank for Fund-related transactions. Disruption to this relationship affects Member ability to remit, receive distributions, and report.

## CATEGORY G · OPERATIONAL & MISCELLANEOUS

### G1. Fund administrator dependency
NAV calculation and LP reporting depend on an independent Singapore administrator. Administrator failure could affect reporting cadence.

### G2. Auditor dependency
Annual audit depends on an independent Singapore auditor. Audit qualification or delay could affect Member confidence and regulatory standing.

### G3. Cybersecurity & data breach
Fund operations and Member portal access depend on technology systems. Cyber incidents could affect operational continuity, Member data, or transaction execution.

### G4. Force majeure
War, pandemic, sanctions, market closures, and other force majeure events could affect Fund operations, particularly gold procurement and US private market access.

### G5. Litigation risk
The Manager, the Fund, custodians, and counterparties may face litigation that could affect Fund operations, Member outcomes, or Fund standing.

### G6. Reputation risk
Adverse publicity affecting the Manager, the Fund, or any underlying position counterparty (e.g., named opening-book companies) could affect Fund operations or Member confidence.

### G7. Information lock-up & MNPI
Members may receive information about underlying positions that constitutes material non-public information under US, Korean, or other applicable securities laws. Members must comply with restrictions on use of such information.

### G8. Side-letter parity
The Manager may enter into side letters with certain Members. *Side-letter policy to be determined and disclosed in the LP agreement.*

### G9. Distribution waterfall mechanics
Distributions are subject to the Fund's distribution waterfall, including a preferred return / hurdle and a GP catch-up. Waterfall mechanics are specified in the LP agreement (intended structure: 2/20 with hurdle and catch-up).
